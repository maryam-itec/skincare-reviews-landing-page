What is Hyperpigmentation?

Hyperpigmentation is the darkening of skin due to increased melanin production. This can result in dark spots, uneven skin tone, or discoloration. The most common causes of hyperpigmentation are:

Sun Exposure

Prolonged exposure to UV rays triggers melanin production, leading to sun spots or age spots.

Acne Scars

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) often occurs after breakouts, leaving dark spots.

Hormonal Changes

Conditions like melasma are caused by hormonal shifts, often due to pregnancy or birth control.

Aging

As skin ages, it becomes more prone to pigmentation issues due to a decline in collagen production and cell turnover.

Understanding Hyperpigmentation and Why It's Hard to Treat

Hyperpigmentation is a common skin condition where certain areas of the skin become darker due to excess melanin. This can be triggered by sun exposure, inflammation, hormonal shifts, or post-acne scarring. While it's not harmful, it can be persistent and frustrating to treat.

Most people turn to brightening creams, exfoliants, or chemical peels. But these often work only on the surface or irritate the skin.

In some cases, they can even make discoloration worse, especially for sensitive or darker skin tones.

This is where LED therapy comes in as a smarter, gentler option.

How Red and Amber LED Light Help Fade Discoloration

LED light therapy uses specific wavelengths of light to stimulate skin function at a cellular level.

For hyperpigmentation, red and amber light are especially effective.

Amber light helps target the pigment itself, reducing the appearance of dark spots over time.

Red light works deeper, calming inflammation and supporting healthy skin repair. Together, they help regulate melanin production and encourage a more even skin tone.

LED therapy is non-invasive, safe for all skin types, and doesn't require any downtime, making it a consistent, low-stress way to treat discoloration at the root.
